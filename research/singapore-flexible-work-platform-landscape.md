# Singapore: flexible / remote opportunity-discovery platforms

Competitive and regulatory research for positioning Discovr Hub, using the "Talent Cape"
brief (US remote opportunity discovery + matching) as the reference model.

Researched 1 September 2026. Every figure below is sourced. Where a claim is an inference
rather than a sourced fact, it is marked **Inference**.

---

## 1. Short answer

Yes, Singapore has this category, and it is more crowded than the US equivalent because the
**government runs the strongest product in it for free**. The generic version of the Talent
Cape positioning is already occupied here, from two directions at once:

- **MyCareersFuture** owns "free, trusted, matched, filterable by flexi-work".
- **FastJobs** and **WorkClass** own "accessible, non-executive, fast, mobile-first".

What is *not* well served in Singapore is **trust verification** and **segment ownership**.
That is where the opening is.

There is also a hard regulatory constraint that changes which differentiators are even
available. See section 3. It is the most important finding in this document.

## 2. The landscape, by tier

### Tier 1 — Government (free, high trust, hard to out-feature)

| Service | What it does | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MyCareersFuture** (Workforce Singapore / SWDA) | ~90,000 active listings. Job-to-Skills matching algorithm, CareersFinder personalised recommendations, MyCareersPlan. Filters include `PART_TIME` and `FLEXI_WORK`, and listings carry a `flexibleWork` field | Free to both sides, Citizens and PRs, government trust halo. It already does "discovery + matching + flexible filter" |
| **e2i** (Employment and Employability Institute) | Placement and employability support | Named in MOM's EA-licence exemption list |
| **SG Enable**, **ISCOS**, **Careers@Gov** | Disability, ex-offender, public sector | Precedent that segment-specific portals work here |

Sources: [MyCareersFuture FAQ](https://static.mycareersfuture.gov.sg/docs/mycareersfuture_sg_user_faqs.pdf),

**Implication:** competing on "we aggregate flexible listings so you don't have to search"
means competing with a free government product that already has the listings and a matching
algorithm. That is not a wedge.

### Tier 2 — Mass-market boards

JobStreet (SEEK), Indeed, LinkedIn, JobsDB, Foundit, GrabJobs. Broad, well capitalised,
not specifically flexible-work positioned.

**Implication:** "accessible roles, low friction, fast" is taken, by funded players with apps.

| Platform | Segment |
| --- | --- |

**Mums@Work is the single most useful comparison in this document.** It has survived 16 years
against free government portals by owning a segment and wrapping services (coaching,
returnships, employer education) around the listings, rather than competing on listing volume.

### Tier 5 — The direct look-alikes, and a warning

Two sites sit almost exactly on the Talent Cape positioning:

- **NextworkSG** — "Remote Jobs in Singapore". Apply via WhatsApp in under 5 minutes.
  Claims every employer personally verified, PDPA compliance, "avg. offer received in just 5
  business days", 100% free for candidates, 3,000+ candidates and 150+ verified employers.
- **ClickJobSg** — "Earn up to SGD $1,000 per week", "Flexible hours: 3–5 daily", "No
  experience required", shortlisted candidates contacted via WhatsApp within 24 hours.

Read those claim patterns against section 4 before adopting anything similar.

## 3. The regulatory constraint that changes the strategy

**This determines which differentiators are legally available, so it should be settled before
any positioning work.**

MOM requires an **Employment Agency (EA) licence** if you do *any* of the following
([MOM, verbatim](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-agencies/eligibility-and-requirements/who-needs-to-get-a-licence)):

> - Communicates with any jobseeker for the purpose of processing any employment-related application by that jobseeker.
> - Collates the biodata or resume of any jobseeker for the purpose of helping the jobseeker establish an employer-employee relationship.
> - Submits any work pass application on behalf of any employer or jobseeker.
> - Helps place any jobseeker with an employer.

Exemptions include:

> - Wholly web-based job portals.
> - Organisations and individuals who maintain and operate any job bulletin board and who do not otherwise carry out any matching or placement.

Penalty for operating unlicensed under the Employment Agencies Act 1958: **fine up to
S$80,000, imprisonment up to 2 years, or both**, plus a security bond of up to S$60,000 for
licensees. Source: [RBN Chambers summary](https://www.rbn-chambers.com.sg/employment/employment-agency-regulations/)

### Why this matters so much here

The Talent Cape journey is "browse opportunities → submit details → **get matched/contacted**".
In Singapore, collecting a candidate's biodata in order to help them into a job, and
communicating with them to process it, is the **licensable activity**. The shelter is the
"wholly web-based" exemption, and that shelter breaks the moment a human follows up on
WhatsApp or shortlists on a company's behalf.

Two consequences that run against intuition:

1. **"AI matching" is the most expensive differentiator to choose in Singapore.** The obvious
   answer to "we're too generic" is exactly the one that pushes hardest toward needing a
   licence. **Inference**, and worth a lawyer's view before building.
2. **The current Discovr Hub architecture is already the licence-light one.** Self-serve
   discovery, candidate-declared interest, no scoring or shortlisting, and the company
   initiating contact directly keeps the platform closer to the portal exemption. The
   compliance-forward design is a strategic asset, not just caution.

If a licence is taken, the **Employment Agencies Licence Conditions** then bite: merit-based
shortlisting, no discrimination on non-job-related grounds, briefing employers on the
Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices, and submitting returns to MOM (jobseeker
NRIC/FIN, company UEN, referral date, SSOC code).
Source: [MOM EALC](https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/employment-agencies/ea-alerts/2020/0622-new-ealc.pdf)

Separately, participating **employers** have their own duty: under the Fair Consideration
Framework, most must advertise on MyCareersFuture for at least 14 consecutive days before an
Employment Pass application, with exemptions for firms under 10 employees and roles paying
S$22,500+ per month. Source: [MOM FCF](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/consider-all-candidates-fairly)

## 4. The trust problem defines this niche

Job scams are **the most reported scam type in Singapore**: 2,701 cases and **S$59 million
lost in H1 2025 alone**, with the primary target age **18 to 35** — the same people looking
for side income and flexible online work. The documented delivery pattern is WhatsApp moving
to Telegram. Source: [job scam analysis](https://lock.pub/en/blog/job-scam-singapore)

Documented red flags include: recruitment via messaging app, no interview, payment for
training or registration, earnings too good to be true, urgency pressure, and vague company
details that cannot be verified on ACRA BizFile.

Compare that list against the Tier 5 claim patterns above: WhatsApp-first application, "no
experience required", weekly earnings figures, contact within 24 hours.

**This is the central strategic tension.** The audience most attracted to "accessible flexible
online work" is the audience most actively warned about it. So:

- **Risk:** conventional conversion tactics in this niche read as scam signals to an educated
  Singapore audience. Speed, WhatsApp-first, and earnings claims *reduce* trust here.
- **Opportunity:** almost nobody is competing on verifiable trust. ACRA UEN verification
  displayed on every listing is cheap, checkable, and directly answers the objection. That is
  a real differentiator, and it is one the government portal cannot easily out-market because
  it is a design and transparency choice rather than a listings advantage.

## 5. Market context

Useful tailwinds, and one important counter-trend.

**Flexible work is institutionalised and growing:**
- **Tripartite Guidelines on Flexible Work Arrangement Requests (TG-FWAR)** took effect
  **1 December 2024**. Employees can make a formal FWA request; employers must give a written
  decision within **two months**. It replaced the 2014 advisory and 2017 Tripartite Standard.
  Enforcement is educative via TAFEP rather than penalty-first.
  Source: [MOM TG-FWAR](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/good-work-practices/flexible-work-arrangements)
- **72.7%** of employers offered at least one formal FWA in 2024, up from 52.7% in 2019.
  **84.8%** offered some FWA, formal or ad hoc. **54.4%** offered two or more, up from 20.2%.
- Top three in 2024: flexi-hours **44.4%**, part-time **38.8%**, scheduled teleworking **38.4%**.
- Singapore leads APAC on hybrid access at **68%**, against an APAC average of 46%.
  Source: [MOM Conditions of Employment 2024](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_PdfLibrary/mrsd-coe-2024.pdf),
  [MOM parliamentary reply Nov 2025](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2025/1105-written-answer-to-pq-on-work-from-home-policies-since-the-pandemic),
  [SBR on hybrid access](https://sbr.com.sg/hr-education/news/singapore-leads-apac-in-hybrid-work-access-report)

**The counter-trend, which matters for wording:** scheduled teleworking **fell from 56.5% in
2021 to 38.4% in 2024**, though still far above the pre-pandemic 6.9%. Fully remote is
contracting while *flexible* keeps expanding.

**Implication:** leading on "remote" or "work from home" fights the direction of travel and
lands in the most scam-polluted keyword space. Leading on **flexible** rides both the policy
tailwind and the employer data. The existing "Flexible work. Future-ready opportunities."
headline is aimed correctly.

**Worker motivations:** salary 82%, skills development 67%, work-life balance 61%.

**Gig context, and a candidate misconception to avoid feeding:** roughly **70,000** platform
workers, about 3% of resident workers. The **Platform Workers Act 2024** (in force 1 January
2025) gives mandatory CPF and work-injury compensation, but **only** to ride-hail and delivery
workers. General online freelancers get no such protection, and CPF beyond MediSave stays
voluntary for the self-employed. Do not let flexible-work copy imply protections that do not
apply. Sources: [MOM press release](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2024/0909-new-platform-workers-bill-to-strengthen-protections-for-platform-workers),
[ILO policy brief](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/Policy%20Brief%20ACTRAV_Evolving%20protections%20for%20platform%20workers%20in%20Singapore_LAY4.pdf)

## 6. Where the actual gaps are

Ranked by defensibility against a free government incumbent, and by regulatory cost.

| # | Wedge | Why it works in Singapore | Regulatory cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Verified employers, shown not claimed** | Displays ACRA UEN per listing so candidates can check on BizFile themselves. Answers the defining objection in the niche. Transparency, not listing volume, so the government portal cannot easily copy the *positioning* | Low. No matching involved |
| 2 | **Own a candidate segment** | Mums@Work proves a segment-owned model outlasts free portals. Unclaimed or thinly served: mature workers 50+, career switchers into digital operations, students and post-NS, people needing genuine flexi-hours rather than full remote | Low |
| 3 | **FWA literacy** | TG-FWAR is recent and most employees do not know how to make a formal request or that a written reply is due within two months. Teaching this is genuinely useful, uniquely Singaporean, and nobody owns it. Strong SEO and content territory | Low. Education, not placement |
| 4 | **Curated flexible employers** | 2,100+ employers have adopted the Tripartite Standard on Work-Life Harmony. A ready-made, verifiable shortlist of employers who have actually committed to flexibility | Low to medium, depending on how "curated" is operationalised |
| 5 | **Skills-first instead of CV** | Fits the accessible-roles audience and the 67% who rank skills development highly | **Medium to high.** Assessing or ranking candidates moves toward screening and placement. Get legal advice first |
| 6 | **AI matching** | The obvious differentiator, and the weakest choice here | **Highest.** Most likely to trigger EA licensing |

## 7. Assessment of the reference positioning

The self-critique in the brief, that the positioning is "still fairly generic", is correct, and
it is a sharper problem in Singapore than in the US for three reasons:

1. **The generic slot is taken by a free government product** that already has matching,
   flexi-work filters, and roughly 90,000 listings.
2. **The accessible-and-fast slot is taken** by funded, app-first players in FastJobs and
   WorkClass.
3. **The obvious escape route is regulatorily blocked.** "AI matching" and "we match you to
   employers" is precisely the activity MOM licenses.

So differentiation has to come from **verification and segment**, not from matching
sophistication or listing breadth.

A one-sentence positioning consistent with everything above:

> Discovr Hub is where people in Singapore find genuinely flexible work with employers they can
> verify for themselves, and make their interest visible without handing their details to a
> stranger on WhatsApp.

That claims trust and verifiability, both of which are ownable, and neither of which requires
a licence.

---

## 8. Recommended next decisions

1. **Get a lawyer's read on the EA licence question before building any matching feature.**
   This is the gating decision. It determines the entire product roadmap.
2. **Pick one candidate segment to own.** Breadth is what makes the current positioning
   generic, and segment focus is the only approach in this market with a 16-year proof point.
3. **Decide whether verification is real.** If ACRA UEN verification becomes the wedge, it has
   to be actually performed and displayed, not asserted. Asserted verification is exactly what
   the Tier 5 sites already do.
4. **Keep the copy on "flexible", not "remote".** The employer data and the policy tailwind
   both point that way, and it avoids the most scam-polluted keyword space.
5. **Do not adopt the Tier 5 playbook.** WhatsApp-first application, "no experience required",
   weekly earnings claims, and 24-hour promises match the documented scam signature and will
   cost trust with this specific audience.
